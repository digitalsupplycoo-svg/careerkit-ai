Most companies expect a counter and build some room into their initial number. Not negotiating at all is usually
the most expensive mistake you can make in a job search — it costs you the gap between the first offer and what
the company was actually willing to pay, every year you stay in that role.

## Before you respond to the offer

Get the full offer in writing first: base salary, bonus structure, equity if any, and start date. Don't negotiate
verbally on the spot. It's normal and expected to say:

> "Thank you so much for the offer — I'm excited about this. Could I have a couple of days to review the full
> package before I respond?"

Use that time to figure out your target range using real data (recent postings for the same role and level in
your area, conversations with people in similar roles, or a tool like our
<a href="/tools/salary-estimator">Salary Range Estimator</a> as a starting anchor) rather than guessing.

## Making the counter

Lead with enthusiasm, then state a specific number, not a vague range you'd accept. A specific number anchors the
conversation; a range invites the employer to land at the bottom of it.

> "I'm really excited about this role and the team. Based on my research into similar positions and my
> experience with [specific relevant skill], I was hoping we could get closer to $X. Is there flexibility there?"

If the recruiter asks you to just name a range, give a narrow one where your real target is the bottom number,
not the middle.

## Handling common responses

**"This is our final offer."** Ask if there's flexibility on anything else — signing bonus, start date, extra PTO,
a compensation review at 6 months instead of 12. Not every lever is salary.

**"We need to check with finance/leadership."** This is normal and doesn't mean no. Confirm a timeline: "That
makes sense — when would be a good time to follow up if I haven't heard back?"

**Silence or a slow response.** Give it the timeline you agreed on, then send one polite follow-up. Don't assume
silence means rejection.

## Example: negotiating with a documented market gap

Suppose you're offered $78,000 for a role where recent postings at similar companies list $85,000–$95,000 for the
same title and years of experience. You could say:

> "I really appreciate the offer. In my research, I'm seeing this role posted in the $85,000 to $95,000 range at
> comparable companies for this level of experience. Given that, would there be room to move toward $88,000?"

Naming the specific range you found (without necessarily naming the source) gives the employer something concrete
to respond to, rather than an unsupported request.

## What not to do

- Don't invent a competing offer that doesn't exist — this can and does backfire if checked or later discovered.
- Don't negotiate over text message or in a group email; keep it to a call or a direct email thread.
- Don't accept on the spot under pressure — a reasonable employer will give you a day or two.

## After you agree

Get the final number confirmed in writing before you resign from your current job or turn down other offers.
Verbal agreements can change; a written offer letter is the actual commitment.
