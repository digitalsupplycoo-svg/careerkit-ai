A thank-you note after an interview rarely gets you hired on its own, but skipping it — or sending a generic one
— is a small, avoidable miss in an otherwise close decision. It's also a genuine chance to add something you
didn't get to say in the room.

## Timing

Send it within 24 hours, ideally the same evening or the next morning. Email is standard; a handwritten note is a
nice touch for some industries but shouldn't be your only follow-up since it will likely arrive too late to
matter for scheduling decisions.

## What to include

A good follow-up is short — three to five sentences — and does three things: thanks the interviewer, references
something specific from the conversation, and reinforces your interest with one added detail.

**Example:**

> Subject: Thank you — [Your Name], Marketing Coordinator interview
>
> Hi [Interviewer's name],
>
> Thank you for taking the time to talk through the Marketing Coordinator role today. I especially enjoyed
> hearing about the shift toward lifecycle email campaigns — it's exactly the kind of project I'd want to bring
> my A/B testing background to.
>
> One thing I didn't mention: in my current role I also built a simple attribution model that helped us cut
> wasted spend on underperforming channels by about 15%, which felt directly relevant to what you described about
> tightening your acquisition budget.
>
> Thanks again, and I'm looking forward to hearing about next steps.
>
> [Your name]

Notice the added detail — it's not filler, it's a genuine piece of information that didn't fit naturally into the
original conversation.

## If you interviewed with multiple people

Send a separate, individually tailored note to each interviewer if you have their emails, referencing something
specific from each conversation rather than one identical message copied to everyone. If you only have one
contact (like a recruiter), a single thank-you addressed to them, mentioning the panel by name if appropriate, is
fine.

## When you don't hear back

Most companies give a rough timeline during the interview ("We're hoping to make a decision within two weeks").
Wait until slightly past that timeline before following up. If no timeline was given, a week to ten business days
after the interview is a reasonable point for a brief, polite check-in:

> "Hi [name], I wanted to follow up on the [role] interview from [date] — I'm still very interested in the role
> and wanted to check whether there's any update on timeline. Happy to provide anything else that would help."

## How many follow-ups is too many?

One thank-you note plus one polite timeline check-in is normal. A second or third unsolicited follow-up within a
short window, especially if the company already told you a specific date to expect an update, can come across as
pressuring rather than interested. If you genuinely haven't heard anything after a reasonable stretch (several
weeks past their stated timeline), it's fair to send one final note and then treat silence as a likely no while
continuing your search elsewhere.

## What not to do

- Don't call repeatedly if the recruiter hasn't responded to email
- Don't follow up on social media unless you already have an existing professional connection there
- Don't use the follow-up to renegotiate something you should have raised in the interview itself
